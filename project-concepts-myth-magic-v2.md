# Myth & Magic v2 — Wonder, Not Fear
### El Behiry × Mardini · Studio, set design, Egyptian myth — luminous register

Director's correction applied: the model/heroine is never frightening and never
frightened. The magic is warm, golden, wondrous — enchantment, not dread.

---

## CONCEPT A (RECOMMENDED) — "HEKAYAT TETA" (My Grandmother's Tales)
**The myths of Egypt as they were actually delivered to every Egyptian: from a
grandmother's mouth, at night, as pure wonder.**

- **Framing device:** a grandmother and a young woman (the model) on a warm
  lamplit balcony/bedroom set — tea, henna, a radio murmuring. As Teta speaks,
  the room's walls open like theater flats and each tale becomes a set the
  young woman steps into, dressed for it. The magic mechanism is theatrical
  and in-camera (flats, painted backdrops, practical tricks), never VFX-spooky.
- **The tales (pick 3):**
  1. **Arouset El Nil** — the bride the river crowns queen: golden water,
     lotus, jewelry as river-light.
  2. **The moon on the rooftop** — the full-moon nights (Layali El Badr) when
     wishes are spoken to the moon: silver-blue rooftop set, laundry lines,
     the moon as a huge practical lamp she can almost touch.
  3. **El Ein, softened** — not a curse but the reason for beauty's armor: the
     blue bead, the khamsa, the bakhour smoke drawn as calligraphy of
     protection around her. Envy never shown — only the love that shields.
- **The close:** the young woman becomes the teller — she turns to a child at
  her feet and opens her mouth to speak as the light warms. Transmission, not
  nostalgia.
- **Why Mardini signs:** the story IS his thesis — Egyptians keeping their own
  stories alive instead of letting others tell them. Real grandmother
  (street-cast face with presence), real oral-storytelling cadence in the VO
  (recorded like documentary), sound design of the balcony night. He directs
  the frame story like his documentaries and the tales like cinema.
- **Why Behiry signs:** three couture chapters with completely different
  palettes and builds, all controlled tableaux; jewelry story built into
  chapter 3; the warmest, most campaign-usable stills of any concept so far.
- **Palette:** lamp-gold and henna-orange (frame story), river-gold and lotus
  green (tale 1), moon-silver on indigo (tale 2), turquoise-and-smoke on cream
  (tale 3). Grain and halation stay; darkness never does.
- **Sets (4):** balcony/bedroom frame set with wild walls; golden-water Nile
  set (shallow tank, warm light — celebration, not depth); rooftop with
  practical moon; protection room (fabric, smoke, mirrors).
- **Deliverables:** anthology fashion film (5–7 min) or three standalone
  chapters for social; stills campaign per chapter; the Teta portrait series
  as the press image.
- **Risks:** anthology can fragment — the young woman must carry one
  continuous emotional line (listener → teller); grandmother casting is
  everything, do not settle.

## CONCEPT B — "AROUSET EL NIL" · standalone, radiant cut
The Bride of the Nile as pure celebration: henna night (gold lamplight,
zaghareet, women's joy), the procession (torch-gold against indigo, flowers on
the water), the crowning (she meets the river and it dresses HER — golden
water-light playing on her face, lotus crown, no submersion, no fear, rising
light). One heroine, one night, wedding-adjacent wardrobe arc, jewelry-heavy.
Pick this if the client/talent wants a single-story film rather than an
anthology. Same golden register as Concept A tale 1, expanded to full length.

## Tone rules for either concept (write into the treatment)
- The heroine is never in danger, never crying, never fleeing. Awe and joy
  are the only two expressions the magic produces.
- Magic is always warm-practical: lamplight, moonlight, water reflections,
  smoke, mirrors, painted flats — nothing digital-creepy, no white eyes, no
  voids.
- Reference blocks A3/C1–C3 from the El Nadaha pack are RETIRED. Keep A1
  (dusk palette, brightened one stop), B1/B3/B4 (Macbeth reed field, shadow
  architecture, tableau staging — relit warm), D1–D3 (the artists' own DNA).
- New refs to pull: *The Fall* (Tarsem, 2006) — myth-as-told-story anthology,
  saturated wonder; *Pather Panchali* night scenes for lamplit tenderness;
  Youssef Nabil (now the PRIMARY photographic register — hand-tinted romance);
  Tim Walker's practical-trick set magic (selectively — keep it Egyptian, not
  whimsical-English).
