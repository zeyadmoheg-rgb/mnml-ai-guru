# "EL WEKALA" — Model in Front, Egypt Alive Behind
### El Behiry × Mardini · Indoor set build · Mamluk Cairo × folk magic · cool & contemporary

Director's brief locked via Q&A: not story-heavy, not conceptual — **a visual
world**. The model owns the front of frame; living Egyptian culture (dance,
music, craft) is the cast behind her. Cool contemporary energy. Iconography:
Islamic/Mamluk Cairo + folk/popular magic. External refs only.

---

## The idea in one line
**A Mamluk caravanserai courtyard built on stage — the model stands sharp and
still in front while Egypt performs behind her: tanoura spinning, zar drums,
brass, henna, lantern light.** No plot. A world that builds like a night out.

## The real-world anchor
This combination already exists: **Wekalet El Ghouri**, the 16th-century
caravanserai in Islamic Cairo where the Al-Tanoura troupe performs inside
Mamluk arcades. We are not inventing a gimmick — we are building the
heightened, couture version of something genuinely Egyptian. (It's also the
scout reference: send both artists there on one night before prep.)

## The signature device (this is the whole film)
**She is still; the world moves.** Model in the front plane — frontal, poised,
Behiry-lit, normal speed. Behind her, the cast runs in half-speed slow motion
(shot high-fps, background action choreographed slower). Two planes, two
tempos, one frame. Every shot is a fashion image AND a culture image at once.
This device is also the cleanest possible split of authorship ever put in a
treatment: **Behiry owns the front plane, Mardini owns everything behind her.**

## Structure — four movements, not four acts (it builds like a track)
1. **EL BAB (The Door).** Near-dark. One brass lantern. A single zar drum far
   away. The model walks the arcade — mashrabiya shadow patterns raking over
   her (Behiry's souk shadow language, indoors, at night). Cast glimpsed as
   silhouettes in the arches.
2. **EL SAHN (The Courtyard).** Light blooms. The two-story arcade fills:
   musicians on the balconies, henna women, lantern-makers hammering brass,
   kids with sparklers — all half-speed. She takes center. First hero
   tableau: full-frontal, symmetrical, forty people in the architecture
   around one immaculate silhouette.
3. **EL DAWAMA (The Spin).** The climax. Tanoura dancers ignite — skirts as
   spinning color wheels around her stillness; zar percussion fused with
   electro-shaabi/mahraganat production. Drums at full speed, skirts at half
   speed, her at normal speed — three tempos layered. LED-rigged skirts
   (real tanoura troupes already use them) give the contemporary edge
   without VFX.
4. **EL FAGR (The Still).** The music cuts to room tone. Full cast stops and
   faces camera around her — one monumental group portrait, held. That frame
   is the campaign key art. Then she almost-smiles, lights snap to a single
   lantern, cut.

## Set design (one build)
Two-story Mamluk courtyard in forced perspective: arcade of arches with
mashrabiya balconies, ablaq (striped stone) painted finish, hanging brass
lanterns as the practical light system, a floor of patterned tile for the
tanoura circle. **One deliberate anachronism per movement** keeps it cool
instead of heritage-kitsch: a neon Arabic calligraphy sign over the door, a
chrome mono-block chair as her throne, an LED strip under one arch. Never
more than one per frame.

## Light, palette, styling
- **Light:** lantern-gold key through patterned metal (Behiry's graphic
  shadow, transposed to brass filigree); deep teal-night fill; one neon
  accent color per movement (magenta → amber → red → white).
- **Palette:** sandstone, black, brass gold; the tanoura skirts carry all
  saturation.
- **Styling:** couture silhouette on the model with street codes (sneaker or
  chrome jewelry moments); cast in real dress, never costumed — the troupe
  wears what the troupe wears.
- **Sound:** zar rhythms and live brass recorded on set, produced into a
  mahraganat-weight track. The drums ARE the edit grid.

## Casting
One model (or artist/celebrity) front of frame. Behind her: the real
Al-Tanoura-style troupe, real zar musicians, real Darb El Ahmar craftsmen —
Mardini's street-cast discipline, performing not acting. Nobody has lines.

## Deliverables
Film 2.5–4 min (cuts naturally to 60s/30s/15s on the drum grid) · stills
campaign = the four hero tableaux (Movement 4 group portrait is the poster) ·
BTS/doc cut about the real troupe (Mardini's bonus piece, and the credibility
armor for the whole project).

## Why each artist wins
- **Behiry:** every frame is a frontal fashion tableau; the front plane is
  100% his — light, garment, pose, retouch. The group-portrait key art is a
  portfolio-defining still.
- **Mardini:** the background IS his cinema — real performers, real ritual
  energy, slow motion, drums; the culture is never behind glass, it
  surrounds and outnumbers the fashion. He also gets the doc cut.
