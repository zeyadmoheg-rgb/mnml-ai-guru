# EL WEKALA
## Director's Treatment & Unified Creative Direction
**A film by Mahmoud El Behiry × Hussein Mardini** · Studio build · 2.5–4 min + stills campaign

---

## LOGLINE
A Mamluk caravanserai wakes at night inside a soundstage. One woman holds the
front of the frame, immaculate and still. Behind her, Egypt performs — tanoura,
zar drums, brass, henna, lantern light — until the whole house spins around
her stillness and stops, facing us.

## THESIS (the sentence both artists sign)
**"She is still; Egypt moves."** Every frame is two films at once: a fashion
image in the front plane, a culture image behind it. Neither is decoration
for the other. The front plane belongs to El Behiry. The world behind her
belongs to Mardini. The frame belongs to both.

## WHAT THIS FILM IS / IS NOT
- IS: a visual world with a build — like a night out, like a track rising.
- IS NOT: a narrative with acts, dialogue, or a character arc. No plot. No
  horror, no dread — the energy is celebration, pride, cool.
- The culture behind her is REAL (real troupe, real musicians, real
  craftsmen) and never costumed, never exoticized, never blurred into
  wallpaper. They perform; she presides.

# THE FILM — FOUR MOVEMENTS

The edit grid is the drum. Each movement is one energy level; the film only
ever rises until the final stop.

## MOVEMENT 1 — EL BAB (The Door) · ~35s · energy 2/10
Near-black. One brass lantern swings gently. A single zar drum, far away,
like a heartbeat in another room.

**Action:** The model enters through the great wooden door and walks the
arcade. Mashrabiya and pierced-brass shadow patterns rake across her face and
garment as she passes each lantern — El Behiry's shadow language as the
opening statement. In the arches behind her: silhouettes only — a drummer's
raised arm, a skirt's edge, sparks from a craftsman's wheel. The world is
awake but not yet revealed.

**Camera:** one slow frontal track backwards ahead of her (she walks toward
us); two locked side tableaux through arches. Her tempo normal; background
silhouettes at half speed.

**Key frame:** her face half-carved by filigree shadow, one gold lantern
burning behind her shoulder, arcade receding into black.

## MOVEMENT 2 — EL SAHN (The Courtyard) · ~50s · energy 5/10
The drum is joined by mizmar and brass. Light blooms upward — lantern by
lantern, balcony by balcony.

**Action:** She reaches the center of the courtyard and takes her mark — a
chrome mono-block chair as a throne (the movement's single anachronism).
The two-story arcade fills around her in half speed: musicians leaning off
the balconies, henna women drawing on hands, lantern-makers hammering brass,
kids with sparklers on the stairs, backgammon players mid-laugh. Forty
people, each with a real action, nobody "posing."

**Camera:** the first HERO TABLEAU — locked, frontal, symmetrical, full set
in frame, model dead center. Then a slow push-in through the courtyard while
background life streams past the lens in half speed. Insert unit: hands —
henna lines, hammer on brass, drum skin.

**Key frame:** the full-front tableau — forty Egyptians alive in a Mamluk
arcade around one immaculate silhouette. (Campaign wide.)

## MOVEMENT 3 — EL DAWAMA (The Spin) · ~60s · energy 9/10
The track drops: zar percussion fused with mahraganat-weight production,
recorded live on set and produced after. The drums own the cut.

**Action:** The tanoura dancers take the circle around her. Skirts open into
spinning discs of color — the only saturated color in the film. LED-rigged
skirt layers (the troupes' own technique) pulse on the drum hits. She does
not dance. She stands inside the rotation — chin level, eyes to lens —
wardrobe and hair reacting to the wind the skirts generate. The crowd claps
the rhythm from balconies.

**Three tempos in one frame:** drums cut at full speed · skirts float at half
speed · she is at normal speed, the fixed point of the world.

**Camera:** a slow 360° orbit opposing the dancers' rotation (camera moves
against the spin — doubles the energy without shaking); overhead god-shot of
the skirt-discs blooming around her like a rosette; low frontal hero shots
with skirts wiping the frame as color transitions.

**Key frame:** overhead — her still figure at the center of four spinning
color wheels on patterned tile. (Campaign hero / film poster.)

## MOVEMENT 4 — EL FAGR (The Still) · ~25s · energy: cut to zero, then 10
On the biggest hit, the music cuts dead to room tone: breath, a lantern
creak, a last skirt settling.

**Action:** Everyone stops. The entire cast turns and faces the lens around
her — musicians, dancers, craftsmen, kids — a monumental group portrait,
held for a full breath. She lets one corner of her mouth rise — almost a
smile, the film's only "acting." Lights snap to a single lantern. Cut to
black. One drum hit over the title card: EL WEKALA.

**Key frame:** the held group portrait. This is the key art, the poster, and
the closing frame — shot as a still by El Behiry on the film lighting, same
setup, large format.

# CREATIVE DIRECTION (shoot from this)

## SET — one build, the fifth character
Two-story Mamluk caravanserai courtyard in forced perspective on a
soundstage (min. 20×15m floor, 8m ceiling):
- Ground arcade: five arches deep on the hero wall, practical rooms behind
  two of them (craft workshop, musicians' bay).
- Upper gallery: mashrabiya balconies, practical and load-rated — cast sits,
  leans, plays from them.
- Finish: ablaq striped stone paint, aged plaster, patterned tile circle
  (10m Ø) at center for the tanoura.
- Practical light system: 60+ hanging pierced-brass lanterns on dimmer
  channels — the lanterns ARE the lighting design's visible layer.
- The anachronisms (ONE per movement, never two): neon Arabic calligraphy
  sign above the door (Movement 1) · chrome mono-block throne (2) · LED
  skirt rig (3) · none (4 — the still is pure).

## LIGHT — El Behiry's plot, Mardini's constraint
- Master source: "moonlight" — one hard toplight bank through a skylight
  grid, cool steel-blue, always on.
- Key on the model: warm lantern-gold, hard, patterned through pierced brass
  gobos — graphic shadow on her at all times. Bounce/negative on her only.
- The world behind takes practical lantern light as it falls (Mardini's
  realism), lifted by the moon top.
- One neon accent per movement: magenta (1) → amber (2) → red (3) → none (4).
- Rule written into the plot: no light source in frame that couldn't be a
  lantern, the moon, or the named anachronism.

## GRADE & TEXTURE
Warm golds and sandstone against teal-black night; skirts carry all
saturation; fine 35mm grain and mild halation on lantern flames; skin real,
garment retouch only in stills. Contrast high, blacks deep but breathing.

## CAMERA
Large-format digital (Alexa 35 / LF), spherical primes 24/35/50/75, speeds
24 and 72–96 fps for the two-tempo device (background plates and skirt work
at high speed, model coverage at 24). Movement grammar: locked tableaux ·
slow frontal tracks · one counter-rotation orbit · one overhead. Handheld
exists ONLY in the insert unit (hands, craft, drum skins — Mardini
operating). Nothing whips, nothing shakes.

## CASTING
- HER: one model or artist — Egyptian or Arab, presence over prettiness;
  must hold a lens without performing. (If a music artist, the track becomes
  theirs and the film doubles as a video — decide before booking the build.)
- THE HOUSE: real tanoura troupe (4–6 spinners incl. LED skirts), zar
  ensemble with lead vocalist-percussionists, small brass line, henna women,
  2–3 Darb El Ahmar craftsmen with real tools, kids with guardians,
  backgammon elders. ~40 total. Street-cast by Mardini's unit; paid, credited,
  and interviewed for the BTS doc.

## WARDROBE
- Her arc across movements: black sculptural silhouette (1) → structured
  couture with metal/chrome jewelry (2) → a piece with movement — cape,
  fringe, or liquid fabric that answers the skirt-wind (3) → the same look,
  stilled (4). All silhouettes readable in full-length frontal frames.
- The house wears its own clothes. Zero costume department touch beyond
  care. The troupes' skirts are their skirts (plus the LED layer they
  already use).

## SOUND & MUSIC
Zar rhythms and brass recorded live on set (this is why real musicians are
non-negotiable), then produced with mahraganat weight — commission a Cairo
producer; the drums define the edit grid so the film recuts naturally to
60/30/15/6s. Movement 4's silence is part of the score: room tone, breath,
lantern creak, one settling skirt.

## DELIVERABLES
Film 2.5–4 min (16:9 + 4:5 + 9:16 reframes planned in the storyboard, not
cropped after) · 4 hero stills (one per movement, shot on set by El Behiry
on the film's light) · social cuts on the drum grid · BTS documentary short
on the real troupe (Mardini directs; it is the project's credibility armor
and his authored piece).

# HOW THE TWO ARTISTS WORK (put this page in both decks)

**The device is the org chart.** Front plane / back plane:
- **El Behiry owns:** the model — her light, lens, pose, wardrobe on camera,
  the four hero stills, retouch and grade of the stills, and sign-off on
  every frontal tableau composition.
- **Mardini owns:** everything behind her — casting the house, directing all
  background action and choreography, the insert/handheld unit, the sound
  recordings, the BTS doc, and the edit's rhythm.
- **Co-owned (only two things):** the final film grade and the four tableau
  compositions — locked together on the pre-light day, in the room, before
  the shoot. After pre-light, no relitigating on set.
- Client/label notes route through the creative director only. Neither
  artist takes notes directly from anyone.

**Schedule shape (indicative):**
- Wk 1–3: build + troupe rehearsals off-site (Mardini) · wardrobe fittings +
  lantern/gobo tests (Behiry)
- Pre-light day: grade reference + tableau lock, both artists present
- Shoot D1: Movements 1–2 (model day-for-night hours protected)
- Shoot D2: Movement 3 (skirt unit + orbit + overhead) — heaviest day
- Shoot D3 (half): Movement 4 group portrait + Behiry stills session +
  Mardini doc pickups

**The one risk that kills this film,** and the rule that prevents it: if the
background swallows her, it's a documentary with a model lost in it; if the
background is dimmed into wallpaper, it's an orientalist fashion shoot with
props. The two-plane device only works if BOTH planes are at full power in
every setup. Neither artist may "win" a frame — any setup where one plane
has clearly beaten the other gets reshot before company moves on.

  is the whole film's spine.

  instead of a van; Ballet du Caire's high-contrast slow motion is Movement
  3; the moulid films are the sound's soul. He has said it himself: "This is
  our ballet." This film stages that sentence.
