# Studio Project Concepts — Egyptian Culture, Built Sets, Narrative-Led
### El Behiry × Mardini · Concept options for the director's treatment

Constraint set by the creative director: shot in studio with set design, rooted
in Egyptian culture, and carrying a real story for Mardini — not "normal videos."

**The core creative problem:** a built set is El Behiry's natural habitat and
Mardini's natural enemy. His public identity is real places, real people,
anti-artifice. Any studio concept survives only if the artifice is *justified
by the story itself*. All three concepts below are built around that rule.

---

## CONCEPT 1 (RECOMMENDED) — "ESTUDIO" · The Hundred-Year Studio
**A love letter to the golden age of Egyptian cinema — the one place where a
studio set IS the authentic Egyptian location.**

- **Why it solves the paradox:** Egypt's cinematic golden age (Studio Misr,
  1935 onward — the "Hollywood of the East") was *made* on soundstages with
  painted backdrops and velvet curtains. Recreating it in studio isn't faking
  Egypt; it's honoring the most Egyptian image-making tradition there is.
  Mardini gets his cultural argument on a plate: *Egypt authored its own image
  once, before anyone else defined it — we're taking the pen back.* That is
  his anger, productively aimed.
- **Story spine:** A young woman (seamstress, usher, or a modern girl — cast
  one recognizable face) is locked overnight inside a derelict Cairo film
  studio. As she moves through it, the dead sets wake one era at a time: the
  black-and-white melodrama, the cabaret musical, the Nile felucca romance,
  the dressing room of a vanished star. Each set pulls her in, dresses her,
  transforms her. At dawn she steps onto the bare stage alone — the new
  generation, camera in hand. Story = inheritance, not nostalgia.
- **Set design (4–5 builds):** cinema lobby with hand-painted movie posters
  (hire Cairo's surviving billboard/poster painters — a real authenticity
  anchor and a press story in itself); cabaret stage with velvet drapery;
  partial felucca against a painted-sky backdrop with rear projection;
  bulb-mirror dressing room; bare soundstage for the finale.
- **What each artist gets:** Behiry — maximum register: drapery, glamour
  light, couture per era, poster-perfect tableaux (his KOJAK drapery work is
  literally this look). Mardini — narrative arc, sound design (projector
  rattle, Umm Kulthum bleeding through walls), slow-motion dance beat in the
  cabaret, and an authorial thesis he'd defend in interviews.
- **Look:** one palette per era inside the shared mineral/grain grade;
  transitions from monochrome into color as sets wake.
- **Risks to manage:** music licensing for golden-age songs (start early or
  commission a period-style score); pastiche danger — keep the archetypes
  specific, not costume-party.

## CONCEPT 2 — "EL FARAH" · The Wedding as Theater
**A baladi street wedding rebuilt on a soundstage as sacred theater.**

- **Story spine:** one night through the bride's eyes — henna and women's
  laughter in her room, the walk out to the alley stage, the mizmar hitting,
  the 3am dance floor where men cry and nobody is embarrassed. Extends
  Mardini's own "Ballet du Caire" thesis: shaabi celebration as fine art.
- **Set design:** the alley as a theatrical cross-section — strung bulbs,
  plastic chairs, the kosha throne, speaker stacks; "tacky" vernacular decor
  executed at couture level instead of ironized.
- **What each artist gets:** Behiry — bridal couture, jewelry, theatrical
  light. Mardini — dance in high-contrast slow motion, street-cast guests,
  live-recorded mizmar band as the actual soundtrack.
- **Honest risk:** this is closest to what Mardini has already done, and a
  studio recreation of street culture is exactly where his authenticity alarm
  is most sensitive. Only viable if musicians, dancers, and guests are real
  and the sound is recorded live on set. Higher chance he pushes to move it
  to a real alley — which breaks the brief.

## CONCEPT 3 — "EL OMARA" · The Building, One Night
**A Cairo apartment building built as an open dollhouse cross-section; the
camera drifts across floors during one shared night.**

- **Story spine:** the night of the radio concert (the first-Thursday Umm
  Kulthum broadcast, when the whole country stopped): the grandmother alone
  with the radio, newlyweds on floor two, kids smoking on the roof, the bawab
  in the doorway — strangers synchronized by one voice.
- **Set design:** three stacked interiors + stairwell + rooftop as a single
  cutaway build; each apartment a distinct period palette. Mardini's GQ MENA
  cover interior (curtained daylight, vintage furniture, patterned rug) is
  the exact texture reference.
- **What each artist gets:** Behiry — each room is a locked, art-directed
  tableau; wardrobe per household. Mardini — ensemble humanity, one-take
  crane/track choreography, sound as the literal subject of the film.
- **Risks:** the most expensive single build; Umm Kulthum estate/music rights
  are genuinely hard — without the actual voice the concept loses its engine.

---

## Recommendation
Lead with **ESTUDIO**. It is the only concept where the studio setting makes
Mardini's argument *stronger* instead of weaker, it hands Behiry the richest
visual playground of the three, it scales from a fashion film + stills
campaign down to covers and social cuts, and it carries a publishable thesis
("Egypt taking back its own image") that fits both artists' ambitions and the
project's prestige positioning. Keep EL FARAH as the alternate if the client
wants energy and dance over cinema romance — but negotiate its authenticity
protocol with Mardini before committing.
